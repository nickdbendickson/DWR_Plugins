description=Finds dams, dry dams, road fills, ponds, dugouts and road / rail stream crossings in LiDAR, hydrography and aerial imagery that are not on the DWR, NID or bridge / culvert lists.
about=Nine Processing tools (Processing Toolbox > DamSafety Finder > Find structures). Fills across streams and draws: walks the 3DHP / NHD flowlines and every draw traced from the DEM itself over a bare-earth DEM and reports every fill that blocks the valley (spill level, height, head, storage, pool), as a dam, dry embankment or road fill. Ponds and dugouts: the Screening tool 16 pond engine over a water-body layer. Crossings: every road / rail x stream crossing, the places a culvert or bridge must be. Open water: pond outlines from 4-band or colour-infrared aerial imagery (NDWI; several years keep the persistent water) or straight from a classified LiDAR point cloud (ASPRS class 9), marked when no water-body layer has them. Build the water mosaic: turn a folder of classified LiDAR tiles into the statewide class-9 water-count / mean-elevation rasters the open-water tool reads. Also: where each pool spills, dug pits and raised ridges (levees, dikes) anywhere in the DEM, the drainage area and expected pipe size at each crossing, dams / dikes / culverts from OpenStreetMap, a score learned from the fills already on a dam list (bagged boosted trees by default), evidence the DEM cannot give (water year after year in the imagery, NWI codes, LiDAR return drop-out, buildings and roads down the flow path), and review batches whose marks give precision and recall with honest 95 %% intervals. Every candidate is checked against the known-structure layers you give (DWR dams, dugouts, Unknown Structures, NID, NBI, NDDOT, USGS / SARP crossings): listed, unsure or new. The not-listed dams and ponds are also written as DWR-style dam records (status Candidate) that DamSafety Screening tool 1 reads. Screening aid: check every candidate against an aerial image; the Department makes the determination.

    0.5.0 - 2026-11-13 (all 15 items of the small-dam research build order, Nick)
      * Each fill now carries where its pool spills (outlet_dist_ft, spill_on_fill, drain_ac, drain_ratio) and one fill per pool is kept (same_pool counts the humps dropped: spoil round one pit, a road across a pond).
      * Above original ground (the NRCS 378 idea): ag_head_ft = spill level minus the original ground put back under the fill, and below_frac = the share of the pool's volume below that ground (about 1 for a dug pit, about 0 for a pool behind a fill). Reasoned from the definitions, not a published method.
      * Shape of the pool (rect, elong, wall_slope, mounds) and of the fill (top_w_ft, side_sum); terrain round the crest (relief_ft, slope_pct); pool_lat / pool_lon.
      * Toe walk stops at the foot of the downstream face on a steep draw (a steep draw could read a fill 2-3 times too high); distances along a line are true metres whatever its direction.
      * Score tool: four kinds - v1 (0.4's 11 measurements), 0.5 (every 0.5 measurement, one clean positive per listed dam), 0.5 terrain (hinge terms and flat / rolling / cut copies) and 0.5 trees (bagged PU boosted trees). MARKS: reviewers' yes / no / unsure as trusted labels that replace a fill's own label. score_note says when a fill is scored outside the training range or in a terrain group with few listed dams.
      * New tool 'Add evidence to fills': water year after year in the pool (NDWI on each image year; the default is every ND GIS Hub year with a near-infrared band - the colour-infrared years are NIR, green, blue), bright bare ground, NWI h / x and water regime, quarries, pasture share, soil rating, LiDAR return drop-out in the pool (blank where no survey reached), buildings and road crossings down the flow path within 1 / 3 / 5 miles (conseq, a second sort) and the 12.5 ac-ft permit check.
      * New tool 'Pick a review batch': a fixed random control, the top-ranked and the near-the-cut cards of the unreviewed pool, each with its chance of being picked (incl_prob), and a share for a blind second reviewer. core/evaluate.py turns marks into precision at k and the share of real dams in the list with honest 95 %% intervals (Wilson, Horvitz-Thompson, DeLong, paired bootstrap).
      * core/dataset.py files every reviewed card with its aerial and hillshade chips (a training set for an image model at about 100 confirmed dams).
      * The water-mosaic tool can also write the per-cell count of all LiDAR returns (POINTS), which the evidence tool reads for drop-out.
      * known_state: every fill near a listed point carries the list's own status (ND's dam list has one). A listed dam the list calls breached, removed, not built, submerged or inconclusive is left out of training and of the AUC, so the score learns only from dams on the ground.
      * LiDAR drop-out reads a cell as surveyed only within reach of that survey's own returns, so a gap between two surveys is not bridged; nested, warped and hand-written mosaics read right, and a missing source is logged.
      * Default score is now the 0.5 trees kind: models/nd_fill_score_trees_2026-11.json, learned on the 66 statewide tiles with the 0.5 engine (7,612 fills, 678 by an Intact listed dam; no reviewer marks). On Nick's 245 marked cards in Wells, McLean and Billings (31 dams, none used in learning) it ranks the dams with AUC 0.89 against 0.63 for the 0.4 model on the cards that were reviewed (+0.26, 95 %% interval +0.16 to +0.36); the 30 top-ranked cards of each county hold 25 dams against 18. Each region held out: 0.85-0.93 against 0.69-0.87 for 0.4's 11 measurements. The 0.4 model still ships and scores.
      * On the 66 statewide tiles the 0.5 engine puts a fill within 300 ft of 494 of 738 Intact listed dams (66.9 %%) against 443 (60.0 %%) for 0.4, with 7,612 fills against 4,455.
    0.4.4 - 2026-11-12 (pond pool from the LiDAR water surface; honest dam label, Nick)
      * Find ponds and dugouts can take the class-9 water-surface mosaic (water_elev.vrt from 'Build a class-9 water mosaic') and read each pond's pool elevation straight from the LiDAR water surface, instead of inferring it from the DEM (flat surface or shoreline). New optional inputs WATER_ELEV and its band; the output's pool_method says 'measured LiDAR water surface' when it is used.
      * The open-water / fill classifier no longer calls a fill a 'dam' on the strength of a flat water surface alone: a dam holds water above the downstream ground (the upstream level clears the toe by more than the valley's own fall). An excavated dugout or a gravel pit has standing water with no fill holding it up and is now 'dry embankment', not 'dam'. (A Wells County blind review of 89 unlisted candidates showed geometry alone cannot separate small stock dams from dugouts and approaches - they share the same head, pool and height - so no size / storage threshold was added; the fill-score ranking stays the tool for prioritising.)
    0.4.3 - 2026-11-12 (Nick)
      * Cancel is also checked inside the crossings intersection loop (not only before pipe sizing).
      * Outputs carry an is_new field (yes when the candidate is new to the lists given) and sha256 checksums of input files in the run_info table.
      * An untagged DEM can take its elevation unit from the horizontal CRS (a State Plane feet DEM with no Z tag) or from a value heuristic, always with a warning to confirm; a DEM with no CRS and an ambiguous typical value is still refused.
      * screening_dams is only dam-kind candidates (and channel-side dikes that hold water), not dry embankments.
      * The Finder still lists DamSafety Core as a plugin dependency (CI requires it of every suite plugin) but does not import it.
    0.4.2 - 2026-11-12 (build the water mosaic, Nick)
      * New optional tool 'Build a class-9 water mosaic from LiDAR tiles': turns a folder of classified LiDAR tiles (.las/.laz) into the water_count.vrt and water_elev.vrt that 'Find open water' reads with its 'Prebuilt class-9 water-count raster' input. The scalable path - build the mosaic once, then the open-water tool reads it a spatial tile at a time, so a county or the whole state is in reach (the raw statewide point cloud is terabytes, too much to read live). Per tile it writes a water-count GeoTIFF (class-9 points per cell) and a mean-elevation GeoTIFF (the pond water surface for a later FindPonds / Runner step); every tile snaps to one global grid so the VRT has no seams, and dry land and the gaps between tiles read back as no water. Output CRS is North Dakota's UTM 14N (EPSG:26914) - the DamIT build uses CONUS Albers (EPSG:5070) for the whole U.S. Reuses core/lidar (laspy for LAS and LAZ, else the pure-numpy reader for uncompressed LAS); launches no outside process. Verified end to end: a real compressed LAZ -> the mosaic -> the open-water tool finds the pond.
    0.4.1 - 2026-11-12 (LiDAR water class, Nick)
      * The open-water tool can read water straight from a classified LiDAR point cloud (.las/.laz) instead of aerial imagery: a cell is water when the cloud classes it so (ASPRS class 9). No NDWI, no sun angle, no shadow or imagery-year guessing - the classification is already made. Give one tile, or a folder of tiles (USGS 3DEP delivers classified LAZ). core/lidar.py reads with laspy if installed (LAS and LAZ), else a small pure-numpy reader for uncompressed LAS (point formats 0-10, so the class filter and rasteriser are unit-tested without laspy or GDAL); it launches no outside process. New inputs: LAS (one tile), LAS_DIR (a folder), LAS_CLASSES (water classes, default 9), LAS_EPSG (tile CRS only when the file carries none), LAS_MIN_PTS (points a cell needs). It can also read a prebuilt class-9 water-count raster / VRT (WATER_RASTER, band WATER_BAND): a cell is water where the count is at least WATER_COUNT_MIN - the scalable path when reading the raw LiDAR statewide is too much (feed the statewide class-9 water mosaic). Reading the raw LAS folder and reading the mosaic are alternatives to each other and to imagery.
    0.4.0 - 2026-11-12 (Wells review follow-ups, Nick)
      * Finds a dike that holds water running BESIDE a stream or draw (within 200 m), which no line crosses - a pond dam built along a channel, not across it. On by default; into the 'ridges' layer and screening_dams. The whole-DEM ridge search finds them too, so it is skipped when on.
      * The downstream toe now carries past a bench on the face to the outlet channel, so the height is not read off a shelf part-way down. On 1 m LiDAR, Absaraka (Swan Buffalo Detention Dam 12) reads a 27.4 ft crest height against 27.5 ft listed; it read ~21 ft before.
      * Open-water tool drops cast shadows in town (dark in every band, not just the near-infrared: a green-brightness test, GREEN_FRAC) and, with an optional buildings / streets layer, roofs, pavement and in-town pools (BUILT). On a town test this cut about a third of the false patches.
    0.3.0 - 2026-11-12 (Wells County aerial review, Nick)
      * New kind 'long fill (road, rail, canal or levee)': a fill that is on no road / rail layer but whose raised ground runs on 600 m (2,000 ft) or more (long_fill_m, YOUR value). In Wells County the top-scored 'dams' were the twin banks of a canal along the James River and a divided highway the road layers miss; of 88 fills Nick marked from the aerial, 43 of the 76 rejected are now long fills and all 12 dams stay dams. New column ridge_len_ft (length of the raised fill along its axis). Long fills are never written to screening_dams.
      * Score: a ridge-length input; models/nd_fill_score_statewide_2026-11.json relearned on the 66 statewide tiles plus Wells County with Nick's 12 confirmed dams. A model from 0.2 still scores. On Nick's Wells marks (scored by the statewide-only model): 4 of the 10 top-ranked are dams with long fills left in, 5 of 10 (9 of the top 20, AUC 0.71) with long fills dropped.
    0.2.0 - 2026-11-11 (review of 0.1.0, Nick)
      * A DEM that records no elevation unit (no band unit, no vertical CRS, not USGS 3DEP files) is refused until Metres or Feet is chosen: feet read as metres gave 25 fills instead of 12 and heights 3.28 times too high. The unit used is logged; a warning when the ground read is outside ND's 229-1070 m.
      * Cancel stops within the tile and the tool fails with "Cancelled" (it reported success on the tiles done); the crossings tool can be cancelled.
      * screening_dams holds no road fill (also 'road fill (ponding)'): Screening cuts road fills to open the culvert (with Screening 0.19.4's Finder input, DWR #324).
      * Every output GeoPackage has a run_info table (tool, Finder version, GDAL, time, every setting and input).
      * Shorter check-box labels (the embankments dialog was wider than the window); a QGIS without the any-geometry source enum no longer fails on load.
      * Version 0.2.0 as DamIT Finder (the same engine).
      * Statewide score model (models/nd_fill_score_statewide_2026-11.json, the default): 66 USGS 1 m tiles in 7 ND regions, 4,470 fills, listed = on a dam layer within 300 ft; cross-validated AUC 0.82, each region held out 0.71-0.85 (the southwest-only model it replaces: 0.68-0.84).
    0.1.0 - 2026-11-11

Six Processing tools: Find fills across streams (core/embankments.py, new), Find ponds and dugouts (core/ponds.py + core/storage.py, the Screening tool 16 engine, kept identical by tools/ci_checks.py), Find road and rail crossings of streams (core/crossings.py), Find open water in aerial imagery (core/water.py: NDWI + dark in near-infrared + width filter, ND GIS Hub 4-band / colour-infrared presets or any ImageServer; outlines the ponds no water-body layer has).
