description=Popup window and Processing tools for dam breach flood modelling with LISFLOOD-FP and ANUGA per the North Dakota Dam Safety Standards: sunny-day and PMF flood scenarios with incremental results, sensitivity, cascades, nested grids, probabilistic outputs, outlet works, consequences, animation, a submittal report, a run manifest and ND Section 5.2 hazard classification with the ACER TM 11 charts.
about=Builds the breach hydrograph (ND REG_05.2026 Section 5.1.1: Froehlich 2008/1995, MacDonald, Von Thun & Gillette, TR-60 or your own), optionally routes a PMF inflow with and without the breach, runs LISFLOOD-FP on a local DEM and extends the domain until nothing downstream matters, and can re-run the flood with ANUGA. Writes depth, velocity, arrival and WSE GeoTIFFs plus baseline, incremental, vector and EAP-map products. Includes sensitivity, cascade, nested-grid and Monte Carlo tools, rainfall-to-PMF, life-safety and damage estimates, animation, a Word submittal report, HEC-RAS import/export, a verification suite, a self-test for this QGIS, LiDAR/NLCD data tools and a hand-off to the ND hazard classification. Runs offline. LISFLOOD-FP (patched build) and ANUGA (own conda environment) are installed separately; see README.md.

changelog=3.10.31 (2026-10-19)
    * ANUGA bed from the finest terrain (ANUGA_ELEVATION, default "fine"; core/anuga_model.py prepare_elevation, core/anuga_worker.py): ANUGA's elevation was the 40 m LISFLOOD-FP model grid sampled at the triangle centroids, so a road or rail crown along an embedded gmsh line kept only 12-21 % of its fill height (2026-10-19 benchmark, DWR-2801/2662/4441). The bed is now read from the DEM source at its own cell size (the same conditioning, refills and carve as the model grid; 2 m when the source's cell size is unknown, at least 0.5 m, coarsened only to stay under 256 million cells) in 2048-cell blocks into a memory-mapped float32 .npy beside the job (blocks off the mesh are skipped; the file is deleted after a successful run), and the worker sets it at the triangle VERTICES (bilinear). ANUGA makes each edge the mean of its two vertices and the centroid the mean of three, so a crown on an embedded edge keeps its full height on that edge and 2/3 of it in the triangles each side; an area mean of the fine cells would spread a 2 m crown over a 20 m triangle. Benchmark, gmsh with embankment lines: share of the fill kept 13-21 % -> 56-63 % at the centroids, 81-82 % on the edges. Vertices without fine data use the model grid. LISFLOOD-FP keeps its model grid. run_report["anuga_elevation_source"] records the mode, source, cell size and sample counts; ANUGA_ELEVATION=model_grid (or NDBREACH_ANUGA_ELEVATION) restores the old bed and job file exactly; NDBREACH_ANUGA_ELEVATION_CELL_M sets the cell.
    * gmsh mesher, embedded lines just longer than the mesh size (core/gmsh_mesher.py short_edge_counts): gmsh cuts an embedded curve of length L into floor(L / h) + 1 pieces, so a 20.0 m embankment end segment at h = 19.76 m became two 10 m pieces and the triangles there (44.06 m2) failed the 45 m2 gate (DWR-4441). Edges shorter than 3 local sizes are now set transfinite with round(L / h) pieces (at least 0.75 h each); the gate is unchanged. DWR-4441 with embankment lines now passes (smallest triangle 62.3 m2).
    * gmsh culvert ends get the default mesher's 25 m2 (gmsh_spec passes culvert_area_m2 = min(fine area, 25 m2) when there are culverts; a gmsh setting still overrides it). They got the 100 m2 minimum before. DWR-2801: smallest triangle 50.7 -> 17.7 m2 with lines on (gate 11.25 m2), estimated time step 0.38 -> 0.22 s.
    * ANUGA worker: create_domain_from_regions' mesh_filename is passed only when its signature has it (inspect.signature; ANUGA 4.0.1 has none), instead of retrying after any TypeError, which also hid real TypeErrors in the mesh build.
    3.10.30 (2026-10-19)
    * ANUGA default mesher, culvert ends (core/anuga_model.py culvert_regions): the refined octagons (radius max(3 x width, 25 m), 25 m2 triangles) around the two ends of a culvert, or around the ends of culverts side by side, that overlap or come within one triangle edge of each other are merged into one region (the convex hull of their corners). Overlapping octagons crossed each other's edges and ANUGA's mesher left sliver triangles at the crossings: Absaraka (DWR-2801, two 36 m culverts) had 92 triangles under 5 m2, the smallest 0.15 m2, which set the ANUGA time step (about 0.02 s estimated; 0.13 s now, smallest triangle 8.7 m2, mesh-only check). Octagons clear of all others are unchanged, so meshes and job files without such culverts are as before (the default-mesher job-file pin is unchanged).
    3.10.26 (2026-10-19)
    * HEC-RAS timeouts on the wall clock (core/wallclock.py): the RasUnsteady compute, the Wine preprocess steps and the Ras.exe -c polling loop measured their timeout on time.monotonic(), which stops while the machine is paused or forked, so a 5 h (18,099 s) timeout fired after 34,749 s of wall time. Elapsed time is now max(wall, monotonic): a pause counts, a clock stepped back never makes it fire early, and every wall/monotonic disagreement over 60 s is logged. NDBREACH_HECRAS_TIMEOUT_S and the max(2 h, 3 x estimate) timeout capped at 48 h are unchanged.
    * On a RasUnsteady timeout the timeout is written to linux_run/run.log and the Runner log first; then the process group gets SIGTERM, NDBREACH_HECRAS_KILL_GRACE_S (default 60 s) to exit, then SIGKILL (it was an immediate SIGKILL). The half-written results HDF is kept as <name>.p01.partial.hdf (never used as results; copied to _hecras/), with linux_run/timeout.json, _hecras/hecras_stage.json (status timeout) and run_report.json 'hecras_timeout' (timeout, wall and monotonic elapsed, clock jumps, signals sent, the partial HDF and whether it is readable). run.log is opened for append, so RasUnsteady's last lines cannot overwrite the timeout line.
    * The ANUGA gmsh mesher's 600 s timeout is on the wall clock too (SIGTERM, 5 s, SIGKILL).

    3.10.20 (2026-10-18)
    * Reservoir bottom carved from the storage curve (RESERVOIR_CARVE: Off by default, Auto, On; advanced; core/reservoir_carve.py, docs/Reservoir_carve_from_storage.md): for LiDAR that shows a water surface instead of the reservoir bottom (DWR-3161). The footprint is the DEM-connected ground at or below the top of dam upstream of the dam (the downstream side the inflow uses; the DWR dam line a wall), within 3 x sqrt(curve area) (600 m to 5 km). The cells under the water sheet are lowered into a bowl (depth ~ distance from the shoreline to a power, solved by bisection) so the volume at the top of dam matches the storage curve within 2 %; never below the curve bottom / breach bottom or the upstream dam face, the shoreline and dry banks unchanged, nothing downstream. Auto carves only when the DEM shows the flat-water problem (pool volume or area under half the curve, or the floor less than H/2 below the top of dam).
    * The source DEM is never written: _breach_model/<dam>_dem_carved.tif, <dam>_carve_depth.tif and a PNG preview; the engines read the carved terrain (model_dem: LISFLOOD-FP, ANUGA, the near-dam refine) and the HEC-RAS builder lowers its terrain by the same depth raster, so the site checks see it. run_report.json reservoir_carve: method, footprint, target vs DEM vs carved volume and area at five elevations, max carve depth; the report warns "Reservoir bottom carved from the storage curve; the lidar showed a water surface" and says when the footprint cannot hold the storage.
    3.10.19 (2026-10-18)
    * ANUGA gmsh mesh (ANUGA_MESHER / NDBREACH_ANUGA_MESHER, hidden): the quality gate is 0.45 x the smallest intended triangle (min(min_area_m2, culvert_area_m2)); it was 0.5 x 100 m2 and rejected Absaraka's good 20 x 20 mi mesh (smallest 49.93 m2, 31.3 deg) without saying so. min_area_tol is configurable (anuga_gmsh or NDBREACH_ANUGA_GMSH_MIN_AREA_TOL).
    * ANUGA_MESHER=gmsh is strict: a rejected gmsh mesh, a gmsh error or the 600 s timeout stops the run with the reason. ANUGA_MESHER=gmsh_or_default falls back to the default mesh with a WARNING in the log and "gmsh mesh rejected: <reason>; used the default mesh" first in Things to check and in its own section of run_report.md. run_report.json records the mesher asked for and used (anuga_mesh, summary.anuga_mesher).
    * gmsh mesh: road (RECEPTOR_ROADS) and rail (RECEPTOR_RAIL) centrelines on an embankment inside the flood become mesh lines (crown >= 0.3 m above the ground 20 m to both sides, from the terrain at <= 5 m). Near-parallel duplicate lines are dropped, loose ends are joined, and edges are split at nodes passing close by, so real road and rail layers make no slivers. Option culvert_area_m2 refines culvert ends below min_area_m2. The default ANUGA mesh is unchanged (byte-identical job file).
    3.10.18 (2026-10-18)
    * Breach inflow on the downstream side: when no cell is half the dam height below the top of dam, the crest fallback (place_from_crest) now keeps to the downstream side the run already found (downstream_direction). Before, it took the steepest step from the crest, which at DWR-3161 (a low fill between two hydro-flattened water surfaces) went 40 m into the reservoir while the flow trace ran north; a trace that steps into the reservoir is started again a few cells out on the downstream side.
    * Side check: run_report.json inflow_point.side_check compares the inflow point with the flow trace from it and the downstream side; a disagreement (trace running back over the dam point, point behind the dam) is a 'CHECK THE DAM POINT' warning at the top of the report.
    * Dam point near a road (within 30 m, the receptor road layer): a warning that the embankment in the DEM may be the road's fill (DWR-3161 is on the ND-23 fill), unless the DWR dam line (DAM_LINES) was given; run_report.json dam_point_checks.
    3.10.17 (2026-10-18)
    * HEC-RAS model (build_model) site checks, from the DWR-3161 HEC-RAS test (36 % volume error): the build stops with a plain-English reason when the breach bottom is more than 5 ft below the ground just downstream of the dam, the top of dam is less than half the dam height above that ground, the ground inside the reservoir outline is never lower than a quarter of the dam height below the top of dam, or the ground below the top of dam upstream covers less than 20 % of the storage curve's area. ALLOW_BAD_SITE=1 (or the option allow_bad_site) builds it anyway and says the results are not meaningful; smaller mismatches are notes.
    * A breach bottom below the ground it flows onto is raised to that ground (the note gives the storage that stays behind), and the breach opening through the downstream half of the embankment is cut to the breach bottom in the terrain GeoTIFF, so the 2D cells at the breach reach it.
    * No dam length on record: the dam connection is the embankment crest (ground within max(3 ft, 10 % H) of the top of dam around the dam point, stepping over gaps up to 300 ft such as a spillway), at least the breach width, at most 3,000 ft (was the whole edge of the 2D area across the valley, 4,612 ft over hills at DWR-3161).
    * Simulation window: the later of the end of the breach hydrograph (below 2 % of its peak) and the run's latest arrival, plus 25 % (at least 2 h) to drain, 6 to 48 h (option sim_hours / sim_cap_hr). The plan keeps HEC's adaptive (Courant) time step.
    * README_HECRAS.md, WINDOWS_README.txt and PACKAGE_MANIFEST.json say that HEC-RAS's piping breach (a pipe that grows, then collapses) is not the plugin's linear trapezoid, and list the site checks; the manifest adds site_checks, sim_hours/sim_how, dam_line_how and time_step.
    * HEC-RAS results: a time series is paired with the Time of its own output block (hecras.pair_time), and hecras.connection_timeseries reads a dam connection's flow and stages from the minute (DSS Hydrograph) block, never the per-profile table.
