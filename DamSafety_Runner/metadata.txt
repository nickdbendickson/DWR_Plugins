    3.34.0 (2026-11-20; Boann gains a 2nd-order discontinuous-Galerkin solver (RKDG2), a new 'Flood engine' choice beside the ACC and the 1st/2nd-order shock-capturing finite-volume solvers. It is the 'slope-decoupled' RKDG2 of Kesserwani et al. 2018 (the scheme in LISFLOOD-FP 8.0): each cell carries a planar expansion - an element average and an x- and a y-slope coefficient - EVOLVED through the Galerkin weak form rather than reconstructed from the neighbours (MUSCL), on the same Metal/CPU kernel machinery. It holds a lake at rest and reproduces the analytical Ritter dam break, and runs the HLLC flux with the same Audusse well-balancing as the finite-volume solvers. Choose it with 'Flood engine' > 'GPU engine, discontinuous Galerkin 2nd order', or the word 'dg2' (or 'gpu-dg2' / 'rkdg2' / 'galerkin') wherever a LISFLOOD-FP executable is asked for. Its local slope limiter (LISFLOOD-FP's 'limitslopes' family) is ON by default: Boann's float32 DG2 needs it to hold a lake at rest, where the unlimited scheme slowly amplifies slope round-off at standing water; 'nolimitslopes' in the .par runs the raw unlimited scheme (sharper on dynamic flows, not for long near-rest ponding). DG2 uses a CFL of 1/3 (the RKDG2 stability limit) so it takes more, shorter steps than the finite-volume schemes. Reports, the run manifest and the classification record name it Boann, discontinuous Galerkin 2nd order and say it is not LISFLOOD-FP. The three modal coefficients of each variable are stored packed to stay inside Metal's 31-slot buffer cap. No change to LISFLOOD-FP, ACC, built-in or finite-volume runs.)
